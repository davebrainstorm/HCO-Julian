HCO — Identity SystemIntroduction
Introduction
The mountain,
measured.

HCO helps farmers, ranchers and agricultural businesses understand their land and their operation. The identity works the way the work does: observe first, then report clearly. The symbol is a measured section of terrain. The letters are drawn on the same pixel grid. The colours report elevation.

Measured, not drawn

The symbol is eight samples of a real reading of the terrain model, not a picture of a mountain.

One module, one pen

A sample, a letter stroke and the layout unit are the same size: 1M. Letters are drawn in half-module pixels.

Colour is data

Colour encodes elevation or status. It is never decoration, and it lives on the dark ground.

HCO — Identity SystemOrigin
Origin
Every mark begins
as a measurement.

We cut a section through the terrain, averaged the surface into eight samples and rounded each to a whole module. The result is the symbol. It is not a drawing of a mountain; it is a reading of one.

01Terrain
An illustrative model, 96 × 96 samples.
02Section
One cut, line A–A, across the summit.
03Samples
The surface averaged into eight intervals.
04Symbol
Each mean rounded to whole modules: 0 1 1 2 3 4 3 2.

Procedural terrain for illustration. No real location is depicted or implied.

01Fixed sequence
0 1 1 2 3 4 3 2. The samples are data and are never redrawn or reordered.
02Square cells
Cells are square, unrounded and unoutlined. The 0.1M gap closes to zero at pixel sizes.
03Colour on dark
The full-colour symbol sits on Basalt or Moss only. On light grounds use the one-colour version.
HCO — Identity SystemWordmark
Wordmark
HCO, drawn in pixels.

A custom wordmark on a grid of half-module pixels. Stems are two pixels, curves step in single pixels, and the crossbar sits high: the horizon seen from high ground.

01One pen
Every stroke is 2 px = 1M, the width of one sample in the symbol.
02One horizon
The crossbar and the C’s upper jaw end on one line, 0.6 of the cap height above the baseline.
03No overshoot
Pixels do not overshoot: H, C and O share one cap line and one baseline. Spacing is 2 px and 1 px.
HCO — Identity SystemSignature
Signature
Symbol and wordmark, 24 × 5 modules.

The symbol and HCO share cap line and baseline, one module apart. The descriptor is fitted to the ink width of HCO and hangs one letter-pixel below it.

01Clear space
2M on every side, measured from the ink. Nothing enters it: not text, not edges, not imagery detail.
02Descriptor
High Country Observations, tracked uppercase, outlined. It is part of the artwork, never retyped.
03Name check
“Group” or “Consulting” is not shown until the client confirms the trading name.

HCO — Identity SystemFamily and use
Rules
Eight things the mark never does.
01Never

redraw, smooth or reorder the samples.

02Never

use the full-colour symbol on light grounds.

03Never

round, outline, bevel or shadow the cells.

04Never

recolour cells outside the elevation ramp.

05Never

stretch, condense or rotate the signature.

06Never

retype HCO in a typeface; use the artwork.

07Never

add a drawn mountain, sun or horizon.

08Never

set the mark over busy imagery without a quiet field.

HCO — Identity SystemDecisions
Decisions
Six decisions before Stage 2.
Approve the direction

Ridgeline symbol, pixel HCO and pixel numerals, for refinement in Stage 2.

Confirm the trading name

The old logo says “Group”; the flyer says “Consulting”. The descriptor shows High Country Observations only until this is settled.

Choose the typeface

Instrument Sans (open licence, no cost) or a licensed commercial grotesk. Licence terms are checked before release; no font files are distributed.

Decide on terrain data

Keep the illustrative model, or supply a real area and licensed elevation data. Imagery is never presented as survey output.

Check the name and mark

“High Country” is also used by others, including a Chevrolet Silverado trim and an outdoor retailer. Clearance needs a qualified trademark adviser.

Review resemblance

Pixel wordmarks are common in technology brands. A resemblance search on the final artwork is part of Stage 2; it is not legal clearance.
